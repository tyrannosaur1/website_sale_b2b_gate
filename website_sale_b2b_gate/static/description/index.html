B2B Price Gate & Quote Requests
Keep prices and add-to-cart away from visitors who are not your customers, and turn them into quote requests you can answer.
The problem it solves
A wholesale shop publishes its catalogue, not its price list. Retail visitors should be able to read what you sell, and should not be able to buy at retail, but a visit that ends in nothing is worse than a visit that ends in an enquiry. This module draws the line where you want it and gives the visitor somewhere to go.
Three modes, one switch
Hide prices only
Prices disappear from the catalogue. The cart still works and shows its own prices, so a customer can order without a quote.
Hide prices and cart
Prices and add-to-cart are both closed. The add-to-cart routes refuse the product instead of answering 200, and checkout is refused for a cart that holds one.
Whole shop behind a login
Every /shop page sends an anonymous visitor to the login screen. Once signed in, the shop behaves normally.
Every place a price can escape
Hiding the price block is the visible half. A module that stops there still answers the same question through five other doors, and the merchant believes the site is closed. Each of these is closed here, and each has a test that fails if it is reopened:
✓Product page: the price element is removed from the document, not hidden with a class
✓Shop list and search results: the tile shows "Price on request"
✓Search suggestions: the price line is dropped from the suggestion payload
✓Structured data: no offer and no price in the markup search engines read
✓Price filter: the slider is gone and ?min_price is inert instead of a search tool
✓Variant selector: the price is scrubbed from the answer its RPC route gives
Who is allowed to see what
Choose the customer groups that see the shop as usual. A visitor who is not a member of one of them is gated; a signed-in customer who is not either is gated too. Internal users always see prices, so the merchant can check the site without a second account. Leave the group list empty to show prices to nobody but your own staff.
Per product and per category
A product can state its own policy: inherit, always show, always hide. Inheriting asks the public categories, then the website; a policy set on a parent category covers its whole subtree, so a catalogue of four hundred leaves is configured once. A product that sits in several categories follows the strictest one. The policy only applies while the gate is on -- a product marked "always hide" does not hide its price on a site that has no gate.
Quote requests
The product page offers a quote button in place of the price, and the form is served outside /shop so it is reachable even when the whole shop is behind a login. A submission creates a request with its product and quantity, and mails it to the address you configure. The same address sending twice within five minutes gets one request and one email: a double click is not a second lead. The requests are listed in Sales › Quote Requests with a status you can move as you answer them.
Worth knowing

In "hide prices only", the cart and the checkout keep their prices on purpose -- that mode exists so a customer can order without reading the catalogue. On a site where that is not acceptable, use "hide prices and cart".

A gated product's page stays reachable by its direct URL: the page is not hidden, only the price and the buy button. A visitor can always read the description and ask for a quotation.

On 16 and 17 the shop caches the rendered product page and grid in memory, shared by every visitor. The module adds its answer to those cache keys, so a visitor and an allowed customer never receive each other's page.
